I can also share some post-launch stories. After launching the upsells, we faced one issue: read-after-write consistency was not there. As soon as somebody signed up for a Prime upsell, such as enabling auto-renew, they were still shown the upsell again for a short moment, because the database or the eligibility cache took time to update after the sign-up. Cache invalidation was eventually consistent, which meant customers saw the upsell again. This was a bad customer experience, even though it lasted only one refresh. I had to fix this issue. I investigated the possible approaches. One was an atomic cache invalidation, which would require taking a lock across two different services, and that could have caused consistency issues. The other was to reduce latency by making a database call only for requests after sign-up. Instead of reading the data from the cache, I read it from the database only for those particular sign-ups, meaning only for the presentations that were redirected after sign-up. I routed those requests to the database. This added a little latency, but it showed the customer a consistent view. The number of reads for this use case was relatively low, so the latency impact was not on all customers. It applied only to customers who had just signed up, turned auto-renew off, bought a plan, or performed any other action. I was able to show the eventual view. A distributed lock was out of the question, because it could have caused an inconsistent database and indirectly increased the latency of other services. That is why we did not want to take the lock, and the second approach was preferred.
