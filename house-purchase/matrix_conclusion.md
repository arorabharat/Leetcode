# The "Appreciation + 3%" Rule

After generating and analyzing the 144-scenario matrix (comparing 1% to 12% Investment Returns against 1% to 12% Property Appreciation), a mathematically beautiful and perfectly linear relationship emerges. 

If you look closely at the diagonal "breakeven" line where the winner flips from **Buy** to **Rent**, the rule of thumb becomes crystal clear:

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **For the Renter to win in this highly penalized real-world model, their Investment Return must be at least 3% higher than the Apartment's Appreciation Rate.**

### The Data Proof
*   If Appreciation is **3%**, Renter only wins if Investments hit **6%** or more.
*   If Appreciation is **5%**, Renter only wins if Investments hit **8%** or more.
*   If Appreciation is **6%**, Renter only wins if Investments hit **9%** or more.
*   If Appreciation is **8%**, Renter only wins if Investments hit **11%** or more.

### What does this mean in real life?
If you believe that Nifty50 or Equity Mutual Funds will realistically give you a **10%** post-tax return over the next 20 years, then buying a house is only a bad financial decision if you expect the house to appreciate at **7% or less**. 

If you believe the house will appreciate at **8% or more**, the **Buyer wins**, even if the stock market is generating a solid 10% return!

### The Power of Leverage
Why does the buyer win even with lower appreciation percentages? 

**Leverage.** 
The stock market investor (Renter) is earning 10%, but only on the relatively small SIP amounts they put in every month. 

The home buyer, however, is earning 8% on the *entire ₹2.4 Crore asset from Day 1*, even though they only put down 20% of their own money. 

> [!NOTE]
> The **3% gap** is essentially the mathematical point where the massive advantage of leveraging bank money is finally neutralized by the cost of the home loan's 8.5% interest rate.
