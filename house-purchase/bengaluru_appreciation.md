# Realistic Property Appreciation in Bengaluru

When using the Buy vs Rent Simulator, accurately modeling the **Property Appreciation (%)** is critical. If you are buying a **Ready-To-Move-In (RTM) apartment** in Bengaluru, the realistic, long-term (20-year) appreciation rate is typically **4.5% to 5.5% CAGR**. 

Here is exactly why it is so low compared to the 10%+ returns seen in the early 2000s, and why 7%+ is extremely unlikely over a 20-year horizon for a high-rise flat:

## 1. The "Ready-to-Move" Premium
When you buy an RTM flat, you are buying at the absolute peak of its primary market price. The builder has already captured the 20-30% "construction risk" appreciation that happens between the launch phase and completion. Because you are starting at a higher base price, your long-term percentage yield naturally drops.

## 2. Bengaluru's Unlimited Land Supply
Unlike South Mumbai, which is an island with zero space to expand, Bengaluru can expand infinitely in all directions (Sarjapur, Whitefield, North Bangalore). 
Because builders have infinite space to build, there is a constant, massive supply of brand-new properties. This puts a hard ceiling on the resale price of older flats.

## 3. The "Aging Building" Effect (Depreciation)
In a high-rise apartment, your Undivided Share of Land (UDS) is very small. You are mostly paying for the concrete structure and amenities. 

> [!WARNING]
> **Land appreciates, but concrete depreciates.** 
> Fast forward 15 years: Your society's plumbing is aging, the facade looks dated, and the gym is old. Meanwhile, a builder launches a brand new society just 2 kms down the road with EV charging, smart-home integration, and an infinity pool. When a new buyer enters the market in 2040, they will aggressively negotiate the price of your 15-year-old flat down because they can easily go buy the shiny new one nearby.

## 4. Historical Precedent
If we look at Bengaluru's historical apartment data (ignoring Villas or empty plots, which perform much better because they are purely land):
*   **2005-2014 (The IT Boom):** 10% to 15% CAGR. *(This era is over; the base prices are now too high to double every 5 years).*
*   **2015-2020 (The Stagnation):** 2% to 4% CAGR. *(Prices barely moved for 5 years due to oversupply and regulatory changes).*
*   **2021-2024 (Post-Covid Boom):** 8% to 12% CAGR. *(A temporary spike due to supply chain shortages and pent-up demand).*
*   **Long-Term 20-Year Blended Average:** **~5% to 6% CAGR**.

## The Conclusion for the Simulator
If you buy an RTM flat in a premium society today, you should defensively model the appreciation at **4.5% to 5.5%** in the simulator. 

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Let's tie this back to our matrix's **"Appreciation + 3%" rule**:
> If the flat only appreciates at **5%**, and you can easily get **9.5%** in a passive stock portfolio, the math strongly indicates that **Renting & Investing** will build significantly more long-term wealth than buying that specific RTM apartment!
